UX & UI review

Tanzania Wildlife Adventures — page by page

The content and the information design are already ahead of the live site. What holds this build back from a 2026 standard is not taste — it is that the site has no photography and that its conversion path does not function. Almost everything below follows from those two facts.

The three that matter most

Fix these and the per-page notes get much shorter. Leave them and no amount of layout work will help.

  • Critical
    There is not a single photograph on the site. Twelve ImageSlot placeholders across six pages render a hatched grey box with a photo brief in it; the other seven pages have no image slot at all. Someone choosing between a $1,700 and a $2,500 Kilimanjaro climb is buying an experience they cannot see. This is the difference between a well-built prototype and a website. The operator already owns the library — I counted 363 distinct photographs on their live site, covering every route, park, island and day trip. Pull them into public/, wire them through next/image, and delete ImageSlot.
  • Critical
    Nine buttons do nothing, and the contact form silently discards submissions. “Pay $X deposit”, “Add to trip”, “Join the waitlist”, “Ask for a price” and “Email me this quote” are all type="button" with no handler. The contact <form> has no action and no submit handler — a visitor fills it in, presses send, the page reloads and the message goes nowhere. That is worse than having no form. Wire a real endpoint (a server action posting to email or a CRM). Until payments exist, make the deposit button an honest “Request this trip” rather than implying checkout.
  • Critical
    Prices are missing on two entire product lines. All six Zanzibar packages and two of five combos read “on request”. That was the right call — the live site’s numbers there are a CMS placeholder — but it leaves the two highest-value pages with nothing to buy. Get the real rate cards from Moshi. This is a phone call, not a design problem, and it is blocking the two pages with the highest basket value.
  • Fixed
    The homepage overflowed horizontally on mobile. The altitude chart carries min-w-[640px] so it stays readable; because it sat inside a grid item, which defaults to min-width:auto, that 640px propagated up and pushed the whole page past a 390px viewport. Body copy and the sidebar CTA were clipped off-screen. Applied — min-w-0 on the profile wrapper, so the scroll stays inside the chart. Worth a wider responsive audit; this class of bug rarely appears alone.

What “2026 standard” actually means here

Beyond the above: real imagery served as AVIF/WebP with blur placeholders and a priority hint on the hero; a sticky price-and-book bar on mobile product pages; per-product structured data (Trip/Offer) rather than only the site-level TravelAgency; visible form validation with useful error text; and a cookie/consent layer if any EU analytics land.

Accessibility is in good shape — the palette was contrast-checked when the brand went in, motion respects prefers-reduced-motion, and focus states are defined. The gap is forms, not colour.

Route by route

Page by page

Ordered as a visitor meets them, not by severity.

Homepage

/

The route picker is the single best thing on this site and no competitor has it. It is also sitting under a hero that is half empty.

  • High
    The hero is text on white with an empty right half. At 1440px the right 40% of the opening screen is blank. Every serious operator in this market opens on a full-bleed photograph of the mountain or the herds. Full-bleed image hero with the headline over a scrim. climb-mount-kilimanjaro.jpg and migration1.jpg are both already on their server.
  • High
    Ten real TripAdvisor reviews are sitting unused. The homepage shows only a count in the top strip. The reviews name guides — Rafael, Patmos, Kihori, Mustapha — which is the most persuasive material the company has. A testimonial band mid-page, each quote attributed and tied to the guide it names.
  • Medium
    No accreditation row. Trust marks belong above the fold on a site asking for a $2,000+ deposit from strangers. The live site carries APTA, ASTA, ATTA and TATO marks — see the inventory below.
  • Medium
    On mobile the picker sits ~700px down, below a headline that runs four lines in an all-caps display face. Tighten the mobile hero; consider surfacing the route tabs directly under it.

Kilimanjaro routes

/kilimanjaro

Six full-width altitude charts stacked vertically. Individually excellent; together they are hard to compare, which is the one job this page has.

  • High
    Comparison is the page’s purpose but there is no comparison view. A visitor has to scroll six long blocks and hold the numbers in their head. Lead with a sortable table — days, summit rate, difficulty, traffic, price — then the charts underneath as the detail layer.
  • High
    No route imagery, though Lemosho-Route.jpg, Machame-Route-.jpg, Marangu-Route1.jpg and KILIMANJARO-NORTHERN-ROUTE.jpg all exist.
  • Medium
    Five of six routes carry an “own data to confirm” badge. Honest, and correct for now — but a row of five warning chips reads as an unfinished site rather than a candid one. Get the operator’s real summit rates, or state the industry source in one line and drop the per-route badges.

Route detail

/kilimanjaro/[slug]

The strongest page in the build. Chart, day-by-day, honest inclusions, group pricing, live departures. It needs pictures and a way to book from a phone.

  • High
    No hero image and no gallery on a page selling a $2,250 trip. Hero plus a small gallery; ideally one image per day of the itinerary, which no competitor does.
  • High
    On mobile the price and CTA scroll away immediately and never come back across a very long page. Sticky bottom bar: price, “from” qualifier, book button.
  • Medium
    No route map. Climbers compare approaches spatially; the profile shows altitude but not which side of the mountain you walk in from.
  • Medium
    No reviews on the page, and no FAQ despite the operator publishing a long, genuinely useful FAQ set. Adding an FAQPage block also earns rich results.

Group departures

/departures

The most finished page here. Dates, prices, seat bars and a waitlist state all read clearly at both widths.

  • High
    Seat counts are invented. Flagged in the code already, but worth restating: “3 of 12 seats left” is the one claim on this site that could not be defended if a customer challenged it. Real numbers from the office, or drop the bar and show only the date and price.
  • Medium
    No filtering by month, route or year across a list that will grow.
  • Medium
    The waitlist button is inert, so the sold-out row is a dead end.
  • Medium
    No calendar view. The live site’s month grid is genuinely better for “what can I do in August” scanning.

Safaris & detail

/safaris/safaris/[slug]

The parks section is a real content advantage — the live site buries that material three clicks deep. It is also the most image-starved page on a site about watching animals.

  • Critical
    A safari page with no wildlife photography. Ten park entries, six add-on experiences, three itineraries — all text. Serengeti, Ngorongoro, Tarangire, Manyara, Arusha and Mkomazi photos all exist on their server, plus lion, leopard, cheetah, elephant and balloon shots.
  • Medium
    Only three safaris. Thin next to the climb offering, and the detail pages have no map, no vehicle photo and no lodge photo despite the sister-camp tie-in being a selling point.
  • Medium
    Every safari carries a “rate sheet to confirm” badge even though the three prices came straight off the operator’s homepage. Verify those three and drop the badge.

Zanzibar

/zanzibar

Six packages, zero prices, zero images — for the most visual destination the company sells. Currently the weakest page relative to its potential.

  • Critical
    Nothing on this page can be bought. Six “on request” cards in a column reads as unfinished rather than considered. Real rates. If they genuinely vary too much to publish, redesign around an enquiry-first layout so the CTA looks intentional.
  • High
    No imagery for beaches, Stone Town, Jozani or the reef, though roughly fourteen Zanzibar photographs are available.
  • Medium
    The seasons section is the best content on the page and is text-only. It says March–May is cheap for a reason, which is unusually honest. Give it a simple visual — a twelve-month band coloured by season.

Combo trips

/combos

Highest basket value on the site and a genuinely strong argument (“the mountain has to come first”). Presented as text chips.

  • High
    The three-leg journey is the product and it is drawn as arrows between pills. A horizontal timeline with day counts per leg and an image per leg — climb, parks, beach.
  • High
    Two of five combos are unpriced, both Zanzibar-inclusive.
  • Medium
    The 15-day and 14-day sit four dollars apart with no guidance beyond “ask us”. Say plainly which suits whom, or merge them.

Day trips

/day-trips

The per-vehicle price ladders are the most customer-honest thing on the site and nobody else in this market publishes them. Keep exactly as is.

  • High
    Ten trips, no images — waterfalls, hot springs and coffee farms are pure visual sells, and chemka, materuni, coffee-tour and marangu-waterfall photos all exist. Move from a dense list to an image card grid; keep the ladder inside each card.
  • Medium
    Two trips show “on request” and are excluded from the planner basket — correct behaviour, but they look broken next to eight priced siblings.
  • Medium
    No duration/price sort or filter, and no indication of which pair well with a climb recovery day.

Trip planner

/plan

Strategically the most valuable page — the live competitor has an enquiry form and nothing else. It currently cannot take a booking.

  • Critical
    “Pay deposit” is inert. The whole page builds to a button that does nothing.
  • High
    No validation and no date constraints. The arrival field accepts dates in the past; email is unchecked; nothing warns you before you reach the dead button.
  • Medium
    The basket does not survive a refresh. Trip planning is a multi-session activity. Persist to localStorage, and make the selection shareable via URL params.
  • Medium
    No single-supplement handling and no currency switch, on a page whose audience is explicitly USA, EU, UK and Australia.

About

/about

The guides are the emotional core of this business — guests name them, unprompted, in review after review. They are rendered as text rows.

  • High
    Nine guides, no faces. “Ask for one of them by name” is a strong promise undercut by a table. Portraits and a line of biography each. If the operator has no portraits, this is worth commissioning before any other photography.
  • High
    Five credentials in a row, every one marked “number to confirm”. Individually honest; as a block it reads as a company that cannot evidence its memberships. Get the numbers, or show only the ones you can evidence. The operator also publishes APTA, ASTA and ATTA marks that this build does not list at all.
  • Medium
    The porter-welfare section is the best copy on the site and has no supporting evidence — no photograph, no KPAP number, no wage figure.

Contact & not-found

/contact/404
  • Critical
    The contact form discards everything typed into it. No action, no handler, no success state.
  • Medium
    No map and no WhatsApp button on the contact page, despite WhatsApp being the operator’s fastest channel and already in the footer.
  • Medium
    The 404 is bare. A search box or the three most popular routes would recover more visitors.

One caution before bulk-copying

These are the operator’s own assets on their own domain, so using them for their own rebuild is straightforward. But some WordPress media libraries contain stock purchased under a licence that does not transfer, and a few filenames here (Screenshot-…, Springlands-Hotel.jpg) suggest mixed provenance. Worth one confirmation from the office that the library is theirs to republish before it all ships.

Sequence

What to do, in order

Grouped so each block is independently shippable.

  1. Make it real

    Nothing else matters until a visitor can see the trip and buy it.

    • Import the photo library; replace all twelve ImageSlot placeholders with next/image
    • Wire the contact form and the planner to a real endpoint
    • Give every dead button either a function or honest wording
    • Get Zanzibar and combo rate cards from Moshi

  2. Make it convert

    The structural changes that turn browsing into booking.

    • Full-bleed image heroes on home, routes, safaris, Zanzibar
    • Sticky mobile price-and-book bar on all product pages
    • Sortable comparison table at the top of /kilimanjaro
    • Testimonial band using the ten real reviews; guide portraits on About
    • Full responsive pass at 390 / 768 / 1440

  3. Make it findable and finished

    • Per-trip Trip/Offer structured data; FAQPage blocks
    • Route maps, safari maps, Zanzibar season graphic, combo timeline
    • Departure filters and calendar view; day-trip card grid
    • Planner persistence, validation and shareable URLs
    • Real accreditation numbers, real seat counts, real summit rates — retire the badges
